# last30days

Produce a cited synthesis of recent community discussion. Distinguish what people report, how widely it is discussed, and what has been independently verified.

## Scope and sources

Resolve the topic and exact date window from the request; default to the last 30 days. Disambiguate product names before searching. Use available search and page-fetch tools; tool names and date-filter support depend on the environment.

Choose relevant communities such as Reddit, Hacker News, GitHub, YouTube, X, and practitioner blogs. Search independent sources concurrently where supported. Do not force every topic through every platform.

For platform-specific query patterns and the HN date-filter API, consult [source-recipes.md](references/source-recipes.md) as needed.

## Evaluate the evidence

- Verify the publication or discussion date from the source. Date words in a search query are hints, not proof that a result falls within the window.
- Read substantive posts, comments, or transcripts behind promising results. A search snippet or video title alone cannot establish what someone experienced.
- Use visible engagement as a prominence signal within its platform. Stars, views, and upvotes are not interchangeable scores or evidence that a claim is true.
- Check whether apparently independent reports repeat the same announcement or author. Cross-platform repetition alone does not establish confidence.
- Include relevant dissent and failure reports. State coverage gaps when a platform or source is inaccessible.
- Do not infer star growth, sentiment trends, or changes over time from a single current snapshot.

For factual claims that affect a recommendation, verify them against primary sources where possible and distinguish that verification from community opinion. No separate research skill is required for ordinary fact-checking.

## Deliver

Lead with the themes that answer the user's question, then include useful examples, disagreements, and changes within the window. Link substantive claims to their sources; use quotes only when the wording matters.

State the window and meaningful coverage limits. Scale the length to the question instead of requiring a fixed number of searches, themes, quotes, or report sections. Never invent inaccessible comments, counts, titles, or links.
